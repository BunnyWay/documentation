<Info>
  **Bunny Stream Live** is currently available in **closed preview**. Features, pricing, and limits described here may change before general availability. Please share bugs and feedback via the bunny.net Discord #live-stream-preview channel.
</Info>

This quickstart guide walks you through the full life cycle of a live stream on Bunny Stream: creating the stream, connecting an encoder over RTMP or SRT, going live, monitoring it in real time, and ending it so the recording is available on demand. You'll be broadcasting to viewers across the globe in a few minutes.<br />

## What you'll need

Before you begin, make sure you have the following in place:

- A [bunny.net](https://bunny.net/) account with live streaming enabled for the closed preview ([log in](https://dash.bunny.net/auth/login?pk_buttonlocation=menu) or sign up for a [free trial](https://dash.bunny.net/auth/register)).
- A **new video library** in Bunny Stream. Create it first by following the [Stream Quickstart](/docs/stream/quickstart).

<Info>
  Live streaming is only supported on newly created video libraries. Your pre-existing video libraries will not support live streaming during closed preview.
</Info>

- An **RTMP- or SRT-capable encoder**. This guide uses [OBS Studio](https://obsproject.com/), a free, cross-platform encoder that supports both protocols, but any encoder that supports RTMP or SRT will work.
- A stable upload connection with enough bandwidth for your target quality (see [Recommended encoder settings](#step-3-configure-your-encoder)).

<Note>
  **Closed preview limits:** **Event** streams are limited to a **maximum duration of 12 hours**. **Channel** streams have no duration limit (see [Choosing a stream type](#choosing-a-stream-type-event-or-channel)). You won't be charged for **live encoding** during the closed preview. See [Pricing](#pricing) for the proposed rates after the preview.
</Note>

## How a live stream works: preview, then live

Every live stream goes through two separate stages, and it helps to understand them before you start:

1. **Preview.** As soon as your encoder starts publishing, Bunny receives and encodes your feed, but only you can see it, in the dashboard preview player. Viewers can't watch yet, and **nothing is recorded**.
2. **Live.** When you click **Start Stream** in the Control Room, or call the [start endpoint](#start-and-stop-the-stream), the stream becomes watchable for viewers. If **Video on demand** is on, recording begins at this point.

Your encoder being connected doesn't start the stream. A stream that is never started stays in preview: it has no recording, it can't be ended with **End Stream** or the stop endpoint, and viewers can't play it.

<Warning>
  **Start the stream while your encoder is still publishing.** Only the part of your broadcast after the stream is started can be recorded. If your encoder disconnects before you start the stream, no VOD is created, even when **Video on demand** is enabled.
</Warning>

## Choosing an ingest protocol: RTMP or SRT

Bunny Stream Live accepts your feed over either **RTMP** or **SRT (Secure Reliable Transport)**. Every live stream supports both, so you don't choose a protocol when you create the stream - you just configure your encoder with the RTMP or SRT details. Both produce the same output for viewers: the same ABR ladder, player, DVR, and VOD recording. The protocol only affects how your encoder delivers the feed to Bunny.

|  | RTMP | SRT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Transport** | TCP | UDP, with packet-loss recovery |
| **Best for** | Stable, wired connections; widest encoder support | Unreliable or long-distance networks (Wi-Fi, cellular, remote venues) |
| **Tuning** | None needed | Adjustable latency buffer to trade a little delay for resilience |
| **Encoder support** | Almost every encoder and streaming app | OBS, vMix, FFmpeg, hardware encoders and most broadcast tools |

<Tip>
  If you're unsure, start with **RTMP**. Switch to **SRT** if you see dropped frames or connection drops on a lossy network.
</Tip>

## Choosing a stream type: Event or Channel

Every live stream has a type. The type sets how long the stream can run, what happens when your encoder disconnects, and whether the stream can be recorded.

- An **Event** is a broadcast with a start and an end, such as a conference talk, a match, or a webinar. It runs for up to 12 hours and can be recorded as a VOD. Event is the default type.
- A **Channel** is a 24/7 stream, such as a linear TV channel, a radio stream, or a camera that never turns off. It has no duration limit and survives encoder disconnects, but it can't be recorded as a VOD.

|  | Event | Channel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **API value** (`streamType`) | `0` (default) | `1` |
| **Best for** | One-off broadcasts with a clear start and end | Continuous, always-on streams |
| **Maximum duration** | 12 hours | No limit |
| **VOD recording** (`recordVod`) | Optional | Not supported |
| **DVR** | Supported, up to 12 hours | Supported, up to 12 hours |
| **Encoder disconnects** | The stream ends automatically if no encoder reconnects within 30 minutes | The stream stays open until you stop it. Reconnect at any time |
| **Scheduling and countdown** | Supported | Supported |
| **Backup ingest, RTMP outputs, watermark** | Supported | Supported |

### How an Event behaves

Once an Event has been started, it ends in one of these ways:

- You stop it from the Control Room or with the [stop endpoint](#start-and-stop-the-stream).
- It reaches its `scheduledEndTime`, if you set one.
- It reaches the **12-hour** maximum duration.
- All encoders stay disconnected for **30 minutes**. If your encoder drops, reconnect with the same stream key within that time to continue the same stream.

When an Event ends and `recordVod` is on, the recording is converted into a VOD in your video library.

### How a Channel behaves

A Channel keeps running until you stop it, or until its `scheduledEndTime` if you set one. There is no maximum duration, and a disconnect never ends it. Your encoder can disconnect for maintenance or a restart, and reconnect hours or days later with the same stream key. The stream continues where it left off.

While no encoder is connected, viewers see no new video. If DVR is on, the time the Channel was offline shows as a gap in the DVR timeline.

A Channel never records a VOD:

- `recordVod` defaults to `false` for a Channel, even when your video library records VODs by default.
- A request that sets `streamType` to `1` together with `recordVod` set to `true` is rejected.

<Tip>
  For a Channel, use DVR to let viewers rewind, up to 12 hours. To keep a specific part of a Channel as a video, run that part as a separate Event with `recordVod` on.
</Tip>

### Setting the stream type

Set the type when you create the stream through the [Live Stream API](#create-a-live-stream), with the `streamType` field. Streams created in the dashboard are Events.

You can change the type later with the [update endpoint](#manage-the-stream), but only while both of these are true:

- The stream hasn't been started yet.
- No encoder is publishing to the stream.

To change an Event that records a VOD into a Channel, set `recordVod` to `false` in the same request.

## Step 1: Create a live stream

1. Log in to the [bunny.net dashboard](https://dash.bunny.net/auth/login?pk_buttonlocation=menu) and go to **Delivery → Stream**, then open your video library.
2. From **Manage library**, click **Add Media** and choose **Add Live Stream** (or use the **Add Live Stream** card on the Overview tab).
3. On the **Add live stream** page, enter a **Live stream title**. This is the only required field to create the stream.
4. Optionally expand **Recommended encoder settings** to review the ingest settings for your target resolution before you create the stream.
5. Click **Add Live Stream**.

## Step 2: Get your ingest details

Once the stream is created, the stream details panel gives you everything your encoder needs to connect. Pick the tab for the protocol you're using.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="RTMP">
    The **RTMP stream details** panel shows:

    | Field | Example value | Description |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | **Stream key** | `bunnylive_xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx…` | Your unique, secret key for this stream. Treat it like a password. |
    | **Primary ingest URL** | `rtmp://global.rtmp.mediadelivery.net/live` | The main RTMP endpoint your encoder streams to. |
    | **Backup ingest URL** | `rtmp://global-backup.rtmp.mediadelivery.net/live` | A failover endpoint. Configure it in a second encoder or profile for redundancy. |
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="SRT">
    The **SRT stream details** panel shows:

    | Field | Example value | Description |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | **Stream key** | `bunnylive_xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx…` | Your unique, secret key for this stream - the same key used for RTMP. It's already included in both SRT URLs. |
    | **Primary ingest URL** | `srt://global.srt.mediadelivery.net:8890?mode=caller&streamid=publish:live/bunnylive_xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx&pkt_size=1316&latency=120000` | The full SRT address your encoder streams to. It already contains everything your encoder needs. |
    | **Backup ingest URL** | `srt://global-backup.srt.mediadelivery.net:8890?mode=caller&streamid=publish:live/bunnylive_xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx&pkt_size=1316&latency=120000` | A failover endpoint. Configure it in a second encoder or profile for redundancy. |

    Each part of the SRT URL does the following:

    | Parameter | Value | Description |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Host and port | `global.srt.mediadelivery.net:8890` | Bunny's SRT ingest endpoint (`global-backup.srt.mediadelivery.net:8890` for the backup). |
    | `mode` | `caller` | Your encoder calls Bunny's SRT listener, so you don't need to open any ports on your network. |
    | `streamid` | `publish:live/` + your stream key | Identifies and authorizes your stream. |
    | `pkt_size` | `1316` | The SRT packet size: seven 188-byte MPEG-TS packets. Leave this as is. |
    | `latency` | `120000` | The SRT latency buffer in **microseconds** (`120000` = 120 ms). This is the one value you may want to change - see [Adjusting SRT latency](#adjusting-srt-latency). |
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Warning>
  Keep your **stream key** and RTMP / **SRT ingest URLs** private - the URLs contain your stream key over API, so anyone with either can broadcast to your stream. You can regenerate the stream key with the refresh icon **before** the stream starts.
</Warning>

Below the ingest details you can also set up the stream itself:

- **Description** - an optional summary of your stream.
- **Schedule start date and time** - optionally set the date and time (24-hour UTC) you plan to go live. This does **not** start the stream automatically: you still start it manually (in [Step 5](#step-5-go-live)), and you can do so before, at, or after the scheduled time. Scheduling is mainly used to show viewers a **countdown** ahead of the stream.
- **Video on demand (VOD)** - enabled by default. When on, the live stream is stored in your video library and is available to watch back as VOD after the stream ends. Only the part of the broadcast after you [start the stream](#step-5-go-live) is recorded.

## Step 3: Configure your encoder

Open your encoder and point it at Bunny's ingest endpoint. In **OBS Studio**, go to **Settings → Stream** (on macOS, **OBS → Preferences → Stream**) and set **Service** to `Custom…`. Then fill in the remaining fields for your protocol:

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="RTMP">
    - **Server:** paste the **Primary ingest URL** from Step 2 (`rtmp://global.rtmp.mediadelivery.net/live`).
    - **Stream Key:** paste your **Stream key**.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="SRT">
    - **Server:** paste the full **Primary ingest URL** from Step 2, including everything after the `?`. The stream key is already part of this URL.
    - **Stream Key:** paste your **Stream key**.

    A complete SRT URL looks like this:

    ```text
    srt://global.srt.mediadelivery.net:8890?mode=caller&streamid=publish:live/bunnylive_xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx&pkt_size=1316&latency=120000
    ```

    <Note>
      In OBS and FFmpeg, the `latency` value in the URL is in **microseconds**, so `120000` means 120 ms. Some hardware encoders and tools such as vMix take the stream ID and latency in separate fields, with latency in **milliseconds** - check your encoder's documentation.
    </Note>

    If you'd rather use FFmpeg, send an MPEG-TS stream to the same URL:

    ```bash
    ffmpeg -re -i input.mp4 \
      -c:v libx264 -preset veryfast -b:v 5000k -maxrate 5000k -bufsize 10000k \
      -g 60 -keyint_min 60 -sc_threshold 0 -pix_fmt yuv420p \
      -c:a aac -b:a 256k -ar 48000 -ac 2 \
      -f mpegts "srt://global.srt.mediadelivery.net:8890?mode=caller&streamid=publish:live/bunnylive_xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx&pkt_size=1316&latency=120000"
    ```

    The `-g 60` value gives a 2-second keyframe interval at 30 FPS; use `-g 120` at 60 FPS.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

### Recommended encoder settings

Use these settings as a starting point for your encoder, depending on the target output quality your upload connection can sustain:

| Setting | 1080p | 2160p / 4K |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Protocol** | RTMP or SRT | RTMP or SRT |
| **Bitrate** | 5,000 - 8,000 kbps | 8,000 - 25,000 kbps |
| **Video resolution** | 1920 × 1080 (1080p) | 3840 × 2160 (2160p, UHD) |
| **Frame rate** | Max 60fps | Max 30fps |
| **Keyframe interval** | 2 seconds | 2 seconds |
| **Audio settings** | Stereo AAC, 48 kHz, 256 kbps | Stereo AAC, 48 kHz, 320 kbps |

Then, under **Output → Output mode: advanced**, set your encoder to the highest quality in Bunny's adaptive bitrate (ABR) ladder that your upload connection can sustain, and set the **keyframe interval to 2 seconds**. Bunny Stream Live ingests **H.264 (AVC)** video with **stereo AAC** audio (carried in **MPEG-TS** when using SRT) and transcodes your feed into the following renditions:

| Resolution | Recommended source bitrate |
| --- | --- |
| 2160p / 4K (3840 × 2160) | 25,000 kbps |
| 1440p (2560 × 1440) | 8,000 kbps |
| 1080p (1920 × 1080) | 5,000 kbps |
| 720p (1280 × 720) | 2,800 kbps |
| 480p (842 × 480) | 1,400 kbps |
| 360p (640 × 360) | 800 kbps |
| 240p (352 × 240) | 600 kbps |

<Tip>
  Encode and send Bunny a **single high-quality feed at your top target resolution** (for example, 1080p at ~5,000 kbps). Bunny generates the full ABR ladder based on your video library settings automatically, so your viewers receive the best quality their connection and device can handle. Live streams support up to 60 FPS at 1080p and 30 FPS at 2160p. A **2-second keyframe interval** keeps the ABR renditions aligned for smooth quality switching and CBR maintains consistent quality.
</Tip>

### Adjusting SRT latency

The SRT ingest URLs use a default latency of **120 ms** (`latency=120000`). This is the time SRT allows to re-send packets lost on the way to Bunny. It works well on a good wired connection close to an ingest point. On a lossy or distant connection, 120 ms may not leave enough time to recover lost packets.

Consider increasing the latency if you see any of the following while streaming over SRT:

- Dropped or skipped frames in your encoder, or a rising count of lost or dropped packets in its stats.
- Blocky artifacts, frozen frames, or audio glitches in the dashboard preview.
- The SRT connection repeatedly dropping and reconnecting.

To change the latency, edit the `latency` value at the end of your SRT ingest URL. The value is in **microseconds**, so multiply the milliseconds you want by 1,000. Leave the rest of the URL (`mode`, `streamid`, and `pkt_size`) unchanged.

```text
# Default: 120 ms
srt://global.srt.mediadelivery.net:8890?mode=caller&streamid=publish:live/bunnylive_xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx&pkt_size=1316&latency=120000

# Increased: 500 ms
srt://global.srt.mediadelivery.net:8890?mode=caller&streamid=publish:live/bunnylive_xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx&pkt_size=1316&latency=500000
```

Use these values as a starting point:

| Connection | Suggested latency | `latency` value in the URL |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wired, close to an ingest point | 120 ms (default) | `120000` |
| Wi-Fi, or a moderate distance to an ingest point | 300 - 500 ms | `300000` - `500000` |
| Cellular, bonded or satellite links, or long distances | 1,000 - 2,000 ms | `1000000` - `2000000` |

A good rule of thumb is to set the latency to **at least 4× the round-trip time (RTT)** between your encoder and Bunny. Many encoders, including OBS, show the RTT in their SRT stats. Raise the value one step at a time until the problems stop, rather than jumping straight to the maximum.

To apply the new value in OBS, stop streaming, paste the updated URL into **Settings → Stream → Server**, then start streaming again. If you use a backup ingest, update the backup URL with the same value.

<Note>
  Latency adds directly to the delay your viewers experience, so a value of `2000000` means the live stream reaches viewers about 2 seconds later. Keep the value as low as your connection allows.
</Note>

<Tip>
  Changing the latency requires your encoder to reconnect. If you're already live and run a backup encoder, update and restart one encoder at a time. Bunny fails over to the other ingest while the first reconnects, so your broadcast stays up.
</Tip>

When your settings are ready, start streaming from your encoder (in OBS, click **Start Streaming**). Your encoder should show a healthy bitrate and no dropped frames.

## Step 4: Verify the connection and preview

Back on the **Add live stream** page (or the **Control Room**), the **Preview** panel confirms that Bunny is receiving your feed:

- The status indicator turns green and reads **Ready for live stream**.
- The **Primary** and **Backup** badges show which ingest endpoints are connected.
- Press play in the preview to confirm your feed looks correct **before** going live to your audience.

<Info>
  **Expect a short delay.** After your encoder starts publishing, allow **at least 15-20 seconds** before the preview playback and the connected **Primary/Backup** indicators appear in the dashboard. If they don't update immediately, wait a few seconds rather than restarting your encoder. We're working to reduce this delay.
</Info>

<Note>
  At this stage the stream is in **preview**. Viewers can't watch it yet, and the public HLS playlist (`playbackUrlHls`) returns `404 File Not Found` until you start the stream. This is expected. See [Troubleshooting](#troubleshooting) for more on playback errors.
</Note>

<Note>
  **SRT not connecting?** Check that the URL was pasted in full, including the `streamid` parameter, and that your **Stream key** is filled in in your encoder's Stream Key field. Also make sure your network allows outbound **UDP** traffic on port **8890** - some corporate and venue firewalls block UDP even when RTMP works. If the stream connects but the preview stutters or shows artifacts, see [Adjusting SRT latency](#adjusting-srt-latency).
</Note>

From the **Control Room**, you can also click **Show Preview** to open the full-size preview player in a separate window and check playback and latency (last ping) without exposing the stream to viewers.

## Step 5: Go live

When you're happy with the preview, you're ready to broadcast:

1. Open the **Control Room** for your stream. While your encoder is connected but you haven't started, the player shows **Live stream not active**.
2. Click **Start Stream**.
3. Confirm in the dialog. Note that **starting manually overrides your scheduled start time**.

Your stream is now live. The header shows a **Live** badge and a running duration timer, and viewers watching the embed or direct URL now see your broadcast. If **Video on demand** is on, recording starts now.

<Warning>
  Keep your encoder publishing until you've clicked **Start Stream**. Anything sent while the stream is in preview isn't recorded, and a stream that was never started can't be ended or turned into a VOD.
</Warning>

<Info>
  Allow another **15-20 seconds** after starting for playback to become available to viewers. During this time the playlist may still return `404`.
</Info>

## Step 6: Monitor your stream

While you're live, the **Control Room** surfaces real-time statistics so you can keep an eye on performance and audience:

- **Duration** - elapsed live time.
- **Status** - connection health, last ping, and which of Primary/Backup ingest is active.
- **Live CDN bandwidth** - data served so far.

<Note>
  If your primary ingest drops, Bunny automatically fails over to the backup endpoint (and back), so a single connection hiccup won't take your broadcast offline. Watch the **Primary & Backup** badges in the **Status** card to see which is active.
</Note>

## Step 7: End the stream and watch it back

1. When you're finished, click **End Stream** in the Control Room header. This option is only available once the stream has been started.
2. Confirm in the dialog. Once ended, the stream cannot be restarted - you'll create a new stream for your next broadcast.

<Note>
  Ending the stream stops processing on Bunny's side and blocks further ingest, so your encoder is disconnected immediately. OBS reports a dropped connection, and FFmpeg exits with a broken pipe error. This is expected. You can stop your encoder first and then end the stream if you prefer a clean shutdown on the encoder side.
</Note>

If **Video on demand** was enabled, Bunny immediately begins converting the recording into a VOD asset in your library. You'll see a notice that **your VOD is currently processing - viewers can still rewatch the live stream** while it does. Once processing completes, the recording plays back like any other Bunny Stream video, and you can pick or upload a **thumbnail** and edit the **title** and details.

## Getting the embed code or video URL

Your live stream (and its VOD) can be shared or embedded exactly like a regular Bunny Stream video. From the stream's **General** tab, use the **Embed** and **Live stream links** sections to copy:

- The **embed code** (iframe) to place the player in your webpage.
- The **direct play URL** to share over email, social, or any channel.

Paste the embed code into your page's HTML where you want the player to appear, then save and refresh.

<Info>
  For more detail on embedding and player options, see the [Embedding Videos guide](/docs/stream/embedding).
</Info>

## Automate with the Live Stream API

Everything you just did in the dashboard can be driven programmatically with the Bunny Stream API. This is the same workflow - create a stream, read its ingest details, start it, monitor it, and stop it - expressed as API calls.

<Info>
  The Live Stream API is part of the closed preview and may change before general availability. Endpoints, fields, and defaults described below reflect the current preview.
</Info>

### Authentication

All Stream API requests go to the base URL `https://video.bunnycdn.com` and authenticate with your **video library API key**, sent in the `AccessKey` header. You can find this key in the dashboard under your video library's **API** section.

```bash
# Common values used throughout the examples
export BUNNY_LIBRARY_ID="123456"
export BUNNY_API_KEY="your-video-library-api-key"
```

<Warning>
  Your API key grants full control over the video library - keep it server-side and never expose it in client-side code.
</Warning>

### Typical API flow

A recorded broadcast through the API follows this order. Calling `/start` while your encoder is publishing is the step that makes the stream watchable and recordable.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the stream">
    `POST /library/{libraryId}/live` with `recordVod` set to `true`. Save the returned `guid`, which is the `streamId` for every later call.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start publishing from your encoder">
    Connect your encoder to the RTMP or SRT ingest URL. The stream moves to **Preview** (`status` `3`). Viewers can't watch yet, and nothing is recorded.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wait for the ingest to be ready">
    Poll `GET /library/{libraryId}/live/{streamId}/status` until `readyToStart` and `primaryLive` are both `true`. This usually takes 15-20 seconds after the encoder connects.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start the stream">
    `PUT /library/{libraryId}/live/{streamId}/start`. The stream moves to **Running** (`status` `4`), becomes watchable, and recording begins. Allow another 15-20 seconds before playback is available to viewers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stop the stream">
    `PUT /library/{libraryId}/live/{streamId}/stop` when you're done. Your encoder is disconnected and, with `recordVod` on, the recording is queued for encoding.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick up the recording">
    Listen for the `LiveStreamRecordingQueued` (`19`) and `LiveStreamRecordingReady` (`20`) [webhooks](#webhooks). Both include the recording's `VideoGuid`. Once encoding has finished, the video is available through the regular video endpoints, such as `GET /library/{libraryId}/videos`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Create a live stream

Send a `POST` to `/library/{libraryId}/live` with a JSON body. Only the basics are shown here; all fields are optional, though you'll typically set a `title`.

```bash
curl --request POST \
  --url "https://video.bunnycdn.com/library/$BUNNY_LIBRARY_ID/live" \
  --header "AccessKey: $BUNNY_API_KEY" \
  --header "Content-Type: application/json" \
  --data '{
    "title": "My first live stream",
    "recordVod": true,
    "enableCountdown": true,
    "scheduledStartTime": "2026-01-15T18:00:00Z"
  }'
```

Key fields in the request body (`CreateLiveStreamModel`):

| Field | Type | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `title` | string | The title of the live stream. |
| `description` | string | Optional description. |
| `collectionId` | string | Collection the stream belongs to. |
| `streamType` | integer | `0` for an **Event** (default) or `1` for a **Channel**. See [Choosing a stream type](#choosing-a-stream-type-event-or-channel). |
| `recordVod` | boolean | If `true`, the part of the stream after it was started is converted to a VOD when it stops. Defaults to your video library setting for an Event, and to `false` for a Channel. A Channel can't set it to `true`. |
| `scheduledStartTime` | date-time | Scheduled start (UTC). Viewers can only watch after this time unless you start manually. |
| `scheduledEndTime` | date-time | Scheduled end (UTC). |
| `enableCountdown` | boolean | Show a countdown in the player before a scheduled start. |
| `public` | boolean | Not currently used and has no effect. Playback access is controlled by your video library's [security settings](#additional-features). |
| `dvrEnabled` | boolean | Enable DVR for the stream. |
| `dvrWindowSeconds` | integer | DVR window in seconds. Required when `dvrEnabled` is `true`; max is `43200` (12 hours). |
| `rtmpOutputs` | array | Up to 4 RTMP outputs to forward the incoming stream to (each with `endpoint` and `streamKey`). |
| `preStreamTrailerVideoId` | string | Video ID of a trailer to play before the stream starts. |

### Get your ingest details

The create response (and `GET /library/{libraryId}/live/{streamId}`) returns a `LiveStreamModel` containing everything your encoder needs, for both RTMP and SRT:

```json
{
  "guid": "019fed6c-d433-7040-92cd-dfd0e4221757",
  "title": "My first live stream",
  "status": 1,
  "streamType": 0,
  "streamKey": "bunnylive_xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx",
  "playbackUrlHls": "https://.../playlist.m3u8",
  "recordVod": true,
  "availableResolutions": "240p,360p,480p,720p,1080p",
  "ingestEndpoints": {
    "rtmp": {
      "primaryIngestUrl": "rtmp://global.rtmp.mediadelivery.net/live",
      "backupIngestUrl": "rtmp://global-backup.rtmp.mediadelivery.net/live"
    },
    "srt": {
      "primaryIngestUrl": "srt://global.srt.mediadelivery.net:8890?mode=caller&streamid=publish:live/bunnylive_xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx&pkt_size=1316&latency=120000",
      "backupIngestUrl": "srt://global-backup.srt.mediadelivery.net:8890?mode=caller&streamid=publish:live/bunnylive_xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx&pkt_size=1316&latency=120000"
    }
  }
}
```

- **RTMP:** point your encoder's **server** at `ingestEndpoints.rtmp.primaryIngestUrl` (or the backup URL) and set its **stream key** to `streamKey`.
- **SRT:** point your encoder's **server** at `ingestEndpoints.srt.primaryIngestUrl` (or the backup URL), including everything after the `?`, and set its **stream key** to `streamKey`. To change the latency, edit the `latency` value in the URL as described in [Adjusting SRT latency](#adjusting-srt-latency).

These are the same values you'd otherwise copy from the dashboard in [Step 2](#step-2-get-your-ingest-details). Use `playbackUrlHls` for playback. The playlist returns `404` until the stream has been started and has finished starting up (see [Troubleshooting](#troubleshooting)).

<Note>
  Save the `guid` returned here - it's the `streamId` used in every subsequent call. Don't confuse it with the SRT `streamid` URL parameter, which carries your stream key to Bunny.
</Note>

### Start and stop the stream

Once your encoder is publishing, mark the stream live so viewers can watch. Starting via the API is the equivalent of clicking **Start Stream** in the Control Room and overrides any scheduled start time. An encoder being connected does **not** start the stream by itself: until you call `/start`, the stream stays in preview.

<CodeGroup>

```bash Start
curl --request PUT \
  --url "https://video.bunnycdn.com/library/$BUNNY_LIBRARY_ID/live/$STREAM_ID/start" \
  --header "AccessKey: $BUNNY_API_KEY"
```

```bash Stop
curl --request PUT \
  --url "https://video.bunnycdn.com/library/$BUNNY_LIBRARY_ID/live/$STREAM_ID/stop" \
  --header "AccessKey: $BUNNY_API_KEY"
```

</CodeGroup>

<Warning>
  **Call `/start` while your encoder is still publishing.** Only the part of the broadcast after `/start` is recorded. If you start the stream after your encoder has already disconnected, there is nothing to record and no VOD is created.
</Warning>

<Warning>
  Stopping a stream cannot be undone. If `recordVod` was set to `true`, the stream is converted to a regular video (VOD) when it stops.
</Warning>

A few things to know about `/stop`:

- **It only works on a started stream.** Calling it on a stream that is still in preview returns `400` with the message `Cannot stop live stream that was not started yet`, even if an encoder is publishing. Start the stream first, or [delete it](#manage-the-stream) if you don't need it.
- **It disconnects your encoder.** Stopping ends processing on Bunny's side and blocks further ingest, so your encoder is cut off immediately. FFmpeg exits with a broken pipe error and OBS reports a dropped connection. This is expected. To shut down cleanly on the encoder side, stop your encoder first and then call `/stop`.

### Check the stream status

Poll `GET /library/{libraryId}/live/{streamId}/status` to drive your own UI or automation. It returns whether the primary/backup ingest is live, how long ago the last ping arrived, and the current duration.

```bash
curl --url "https://video.bunnycdn.com/library/$BUNNY_LIBRARY_ID/live/$STREAM_ID/status" \
  --header "AccessKey: $BUNNY_API_KEY"
```

```json
{
  "readyToStart": true,
  "primaryLive": true,
  "backupLive": false,
  "lastPingAgo": 205,
  "duration": 12,
  "statusTimeUtc": "2026-01-15T18:03:45Z"
}
```

| Field | Description |
| --- | --- |
| `readyToStart` | `true` when the ingest is ready and the stream can be started. |
| `primaryLive` | `true` while the primary ingest is publishing. |
| `backupLive` | `true` while the backup ingest is publishing. |
| `lastPingAgo` | Time since the last ping from the ingest, in **milliseconds** (`205` = 0.2 seconds), if available. |
| `duration` | The current duration of the stream. |
| `statusTimeUtc` | The UTC time this status was generated. |

<Note>
  `primaryLive` and `backupLive` reflect your encoder's connection, not whether the stream has been started. A stream can show `primaryLive: true` while still in preview. Check the stream's `status` field to see whether it has been started.
</Note>

The `status` field on the live stream object itself moves through the stream's life cycle:

| Value | Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `1` | Created | The stream was just created. |
| `2` | Scheduled | Scheduled to go live at `scheduledStartTime`. Publishing can start earlier, but it's only watchable after that time. |
| `3` | Preview | Publishing has started but the stream wasn't started from the dashboard/API - visible only in the preview player. Nothing is recorded, and the stream can't be stopped until it's started. |
| `4` | Running | Started from the dashboard or API; the stream is live and, with `recordVod` on, being recorded. This is the only status `/stop` works from. |
| `5` | Ended | Finished; publishing is no longer possible. |
| `6` | VodProcessing | Ended and currently processing the VOD output. |
| `7` | Error | An error occurred during ingest or live publishing. |

### Manage the stream

<CodeGroup>

```bash Update
curl --request PUT \
  --url "https://video.bunnycdn.com/library/$BUNNY_LIBRARY_ID/live/$STREAM_ID" \
  --header "AccessKey: $BUNNY_API_KEY" \
  --header "Content-Type: application/json" \
  --data '{ "title": "Updated title", "recordVod": true }'
```

```bash Regenerate key
curl --request PUT \
  --url "https://video.bunnycdn.com/library/$BUNNY_LIBRARY_ID/live/$STREAM_ID/regenerate-key" \
  --header "AccessKey: $BUNNY_API_KEY"
```

```bash Delete
curl --request DELETE \
  --url "https://video.bunnycdn.com/library/$BUNNY_LIBRARY_ID/live/$STREAM_ID" \
  --header "AccessKey: $BUNNY_API_KEY"
```

</CodeGroup>

<Note>
  The **update** endpoint can be used with no restrictions **before the stream is started**. It can only be used for updating the title and description after the stream is started. The `streamType` can only be changed before the stream is started and while no encoder is publishing. The stream key can only be regenerated **before** a stream has ended; regenerating it also changes the `streamid` in both SRT ingest URLs, so update every encoder you've configured, whichever protocol it uses. Deleting a stream removes it and all associated resources and cannot be undone.
</Note>

### Errors

During the closed preview, the Live Stream API returns errors in two formats:

| Situation | Status code | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The library or stream ID doesn't exist | `404` | An `application/problem+json` body |
| The request is valid but not allowed in the stream's current state (for example, stopping a stream that hasn't been started) | `400` | A JSON body with `"success": false` and a `message` describing the problem |

Example `400` response:

```json
{
  "success": false,
  "message": "Cannot stop live stream that was not started yet"
}
```

<Note>
  We plan to unify these formats before general availability. Check the HTTP status code first, and handle both body formats in the meantime.
</Note>

| Method | Path | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |

| `GET` | `/library/{libraryId}/live` | List live streams (`page`, `itemsPerPage`, `search`, `orderBy`, `collectionId`). |
| `GET` | `/library/{libraryId}/live/{streamId}` | Get a live stream by ID. |
| `PUT` | `/library/{libraryId}/live/{streamId}` | Update a live stream. |
| `DELETE` | `/library/{libraryId}/live/{streamId}` | Delete a live stream and its resources. |
| `PUT` | `/library/{libraryId}/live/{streamId}/start` | Start the stream (make it watchable and, with `recordVod` on, begin recording). Call while the encoder is publishing. |
| `PUT` | `/library/{libraryId}/live/{streamId}/stop` | Stop a started stream (converts to VOD if `recordVod` is enabled). Disconnects the encoder. Returns `400` if the stream hasn't been started. |

## Webhooks

Live streams send webhooks to your video library's webhook URL, the same URL that receives video webhooks. Webhooks are configured per library, so there is no separate live stream webhook URL. Requests are signed, delivered, and retried the same way. See [Webhooks](/stream/webhooks) to set up the URL and [validate the signature](/stream/webhooks#signature-validation).

<Info>
  Live stream webhooks are part of the closed preview and may change before general availability.
</Info>

### Payload

<CodeGroup>

```json JSON
{
  "IsLiveStreamWebhook": true,
  "VideoLibraryId": 133,
  "LiveStreamId": "019fed6c-d433-7040-92cd-dfd0e4221757",
  "Status": 18,
  "EventId": "4b1f8e0e-7c55-4f7e-9b0e-5d2a3c9b8f11",
  "Timestamp": "2026-01-15T19:30:15.123456Z",
  "Reason": "Manual"
}
```

</CodeGroup>

| Field | Description |
| --- | --- |
| `IsLiveStreamWebhook` | `true` for live stream webhooks. Use it to tell live stream events apart from video events on the shared URL. |
| `VideoLibraryId` | The ID of the video library. |
| `LiveStreamId` | The ID of the live stream. |
| `Status` | The status code of the event. See the [status list](#live-stream-statuses) below. |
| `EventId` | A unique ID for the event. Use it to drop duplicate deliveries. |
| `Timestamp` | The UTC time the event happened. Use it to order events. |
| `Reason` | Why the event happened, for statuses that have one. Left out when there is none. |
| `VideoGuid` | Only on the recording events (`19` to `21`): the ID of the recording video. |

<Note>
  Switch on the numeric `Status` value, not on the status name. New statuses and `Reason` values may be added, so ignore the ones you don't recognize.
</Note>

### Live stream statuses

| Value | Status | Sent when | `Reason` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `14` | LiveStreamCreated | The live stream was created. | - |
| `15` | LiveStreamPublishingStarted | The primary ingest started publishing. The stream is in preview until it's started. | - |
| `16` | LiveStreamStarted | The stream went live for viewers. | `Manual`, `Scheduled` |
| `17` | LiveStreamDisconnected | All ingests disconnected. | `IngestLeft`, `IngestRejected`, `IngestTimeout`, `EncoderLost`, `Blocked`, `Unknown` |
| `18` | LiveStreamStopped | The stream ended. | `Manual`, `Scheduled`, `DisconnectTimeout`, `MaxDuration`, `LibraryDisabled`, `Unknown` |
| `19` | LiveStreamRecordingQueued | The recording was queued for encoding. | - |
| `20` | LiveStreamRecordingReady | The recording finished encoding. | - |
| `21` | LiveStreamRecordingFailed | The recording failed. | - |
| `22` | LiveStreamDeleted | The live stream was deleted. | - |
| `23` | LiveStreamKeyRegenerated | The stream key was regenerated. | - |
| `24` | LiveStreamReconnected | The primary ingest reconnected after a disconnect. | - |
| `25` | LiveStreamBackupConnected | The backup ingest connected. | - |
| `26` | LiveStreamBackupDisconnected | The backup ingest disconnected. | Same as `17` |
| `27` | LiveStreamFailedOver | The backup ingest took over from the primary. | `PrimaryEncoderLost`, `PrimaryStalled`, `IngestLeft` |
| `28` | LiveStreamInterrupted | The primary ingest is connected, but no new media reaches viewers. | `PrimaryEncoderLost`, `PrimaryStalled` |
| `29` | LiveStreamResumed | Media is flowing again after an interruption. | - |
| `30` | LiveStreamBlockChanged | The stream's ingest was blocked or unblocked. | `Blocked`, `Unblocked` |
| `31` | LiveStreamPublishRejected | An attempt to publish to the stream was refused. | `StreamEnded`, `StreamBlocked`, `TooManyIngests`, `RegionConflict` |

The `DisconnectTimeout` and `MaxDuration` reasons on status `18` apply to Events only. A Channel is never stopped for either reason, and never sends the recording statuses `19` to `21`.

If you receive `15` (LiveStreamPublishingStarted) but never `16` (LiveStreamStarted), the stream was never started, so no recording statuses (`19` to `21`) follow.

Statuses `17` and `24` to `31` are best effort, so in rare cases one may not be delivered. To check the current state of a stream, use the [status endpoint](#check-the-stream-status).

When **Video on demand** is enabled, the recording also sends the ordinary [video webhooks](/stream/webhooks#status-list) while it encodes, with `IsLiveStreamWebhook` set to `false`.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="My stream ended but no VOD appeared in my library">
    The most common cause is that the stream was never started while the encoder was publishing. Only the part of a broadcast after **Start Stream** (or `/start`) is recorded. If you start the stream after the encoder has already disconnected, there is nothing to record.

    Check that:

    - **Video on demand** (`recordVod`) was on for the stream.
    - The stream reached **Running** (`status` `4`) while your encoder was still publishing.
    - The stream was an **Event**. Channels never record a VOD.

    If all of these are true, the VOD may still be encoding. Wait for the `LiveStreamRecordingReady` (`20`) webhook, or check the video with the `VideoGuid` from the `LiveStreamRecordingQueued` (`19`) webhook.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title='/stop returns 400 "Cannot stop live stream that was not started yet"'>
    The stream is still in preview (`status` `3`). An encoder being connected doesn't start the stream, so there's nothing to stop. Call `/start` first if you want the stream to go live and be recorded, or delete the stream if you don't need it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The HLS playlist returns 404 File Not Found">
    This is expected in two situations:

    - The stream hasn't been started yet. The playlist only exists for viewers once the stream is running.
    - The stream has just been started. Allow **15-20 seconds** for the stream to start up before the playlist is available.

    If the playlist still returns `404` well after the stream has started, check the stream's `status` and contact support with the stream ID.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The HLS playlist returns 403 Forbidden">
    By default, your video library blocks playback requests that don't send a `Referer` header. This applies to all videos in the library, not only live streams. Requests from a browser page usually send a `Referer` automatically; requests from tools such as `curl` don't. Add a `Referer` header when testing, or review the allowed domains and authentication options in your video library's **Security** settings.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="lastPingAgo looks very high">
    `lastPingAgo` is in **milliseconds**, not seconds. A value of `205` means the last ping arrived 0.2 seconds ago.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="My encoder reports an error when the stream ends">
    Ending a stream disconnects the encoder immediately, so FFmpeg exits with a broken pipe error and OBS reports a dropped connection. This is expected and doesn't affect the recording. Stop your encoder before ending the stream if you prefer a clean shutdown.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Additional features

- Live Stream also supports the following within **advanced settings** and your **video library live stream settings**:
  - **SRT ingest** as an alternative to RTMP, for resilient contribution over unreliable networks with adjustable latency.
  - **Countdown** within the bunny player so viewers can see how long they have left before the live stream starts.
  - **Thumbnails** displayed within the player before the live stream starts and generated during the live stream.
  - **Prestream Trailer** so viewers can watch a looping video with a countdown before the live stream starts.
  - **DVR** meaning viewers can rewind during the live stream a set amount of time.
  - **Watermarking** rendered into the live video stream. This is unique to the live stream library, meaning a clean version is generated for VOD so you can apply a different watermark to your VOD.
  - **RTMP Output** for co-streaming live to other platforms in parallel.
  - **VOD** is re-encoded, meaning all of Bunny Stream's VOD features - such as Premium encoding, Transcription, Smart generate, and eDRM - are supported for the VOD.
- Configure **player UI and authentication** (allowed/blocked domains, token, and CDN authentication) in your **player and security video library settings**. These settings control who can play your live streams.
- Automate stream creation and control with the [Live Stream API](#automate-with-the-live-stream-api) - create, start, stop, monitor, and manage streams programmatically.
- React to stream events, such as a disconnected ingest or a finished recording, with [webhooks](#webhooks).

## Pricing

<Info>
  **Live encoding is free during the closed preview.** The rates below are the **proposed** pricing for general availability and may change before launch.
</Info>

Live encoding is billed **per minute**. The rate depends on the **top output resolution** of your live encode, which is set in your video library settings. It does not depend on the resolution of the feed your encoder sends.

| Top output resolution | Proposed price per minute |
| --- | --- |
| 1440p - 2160p / 4K | \$0.15 |
| 720p - 1080p | \$0.05 |
| 480p and below | \$0.025 |

For example, a library with a 720p top output is billed at the 1080p rate, and a library with a 360p top output is billed at the 480p rate.

### Primary and backup encodes

Bunny encodes the primary and backup ingest as two fully independent, parallel live encodes, so each one can take over instantly if the other fails. Because of this, **primary and backup are charged separately**. You only pay for a backup encode while a backup encoder is publishing.

For example, a 1-hour stream with a 1080p top output costs:

| Setup | Calculation | Live encoding cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary ingest only | 60 min × \$0.05 | \$3.00 |
| Primary + backup ingest | 2 × 60 min × \$0.05 | \$6.00 |

A Channel is billed the same way, for every minute an encoder is publishing to it. A Channel that runs 24/7 with a 1080p top output and primary ingest only costs 1,440 min × \$0.05 = \$72.00 per day.

### Other charges

Live encoding charges are in addition to your usual bunny.net usage:

- **CDN bandwidth** for viewers watching the live stream or the VOD recording.
- **Storage** for the VOD recording and any other files kept in your video library.

<Tip>
  To reduce live encoding costs, lower the top output resolution in your video library settings to what your audience actually needs. Only run a backup encoder for broadcasts where uninterrupted delivery is worth the extra encode.
</Tip>

## Limits

- 12 hour maximum live duration for Events. Channels have no duration limit.
- 12 hour maximum DVR window for both Events and Channels.
- An Event ends after 30 minutes without a connected encoder. A Channel stays open until you stop it.
- A Channel can't record a VOD.
- Only the part of a stream after it was started is recorded.
- 3 concurrent live streams per bunny account during closed preview
- **Startup delay** The preview and the viewer playlist take 15-20 seconds to become available after the encoder connects and after the stream is started.

## Known issues

These are known issues in the closed preview that we're working on:

- **`current-bitrate` may return `404`** while a stream is live. We're moving bitrate tracking to a new backend, which will fix this.
- **Two error formats.** Unknown IDs return `404` with a `problem+json` body, while state errors return `400` with a `{"success": false, ...}` body. See [Errors](#errors).
- **The `public` field has no effect.** Setting it to `false` doesn't restrict playback. Use your video library's security settings to control access instead.

## Not currently supported by Stream Live during closed preview

- RTMPS ingest
- Live statistics
- Low-latency HLS live streaming
- VOD clipping
